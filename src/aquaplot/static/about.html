IEEE OneAquaHealth Global Hackathon 2026 · Track 3, AI-Supported Assessment

A volunteer's stream reading, checked by an AI that never saw their answer.

AquaPlot turns a walk past an urban stream into a health reading for the water, the people and the animals around it. The citizen identifies; the AI double-checks, blind; published indices and readable rules decide.

The problem. The track names it: citizen observations can be inconsistent and error-prone. The animals living in a stream are the best evidence of its health, and the hardest thing for a volunteer to name. A flat-headed mayfly and a stonefly look alike to a beginner, and one wrong name can move a stream's reading.

The obvious fix, letting an AI identify the animals, makes it worse in a quieter way: the volunteer learns nothing, nobody can tell when the AI is wrong, and a machine's guess ends up in a health record.

And a reading that ends in a number nobody acts on protects nobody. OneAquaHealth's own Citizen Science App records the channel, banks and flow, but does not ask what lives in the water.

The moment worth seeing

  1. The photos are loaded.A reach, and a tray with one nymph in it, placed at OneAquaHealth research site C3 in Coimbra.
  2. You name the animal.Search "two tails", pick a stonefly. Your list is what gets scored.
  3. The AI asks, it doesn't overrule.It looked at the tray without your answer, and only raises a question where it saw something different.

"You marked some kind of stonefly. The model thinks this may be Mayfly (flat-headed). Which is it?"
How to tell: "Count the tails and look at the sides of the body. Mayfly nymphs usually have three tails and feathery or plate-like gills along the sides of the abdomen; stonefly nymphs have two tails and no gills on the abdomen."

You decide. Because the model was asked blind, its agreement is independent evidence rather than an echo, and taking its answer is recorded as that, never as agreement. Questions are ranked by whether the answer would change the stream's reading.

What happens to the reading

A One Health read-out, with its reasons

  • A Water Framework Directive screening band from a published index (BMWP, or the Iberian IBMWP in Portugal and Spain, chosen from the coordinates).
  • Findings for ecosystem, human and animal health, each naming its rule, its evidence and an action.
  • A health alert waits until a person confirms the observation under it.

Something to send, in the project's own formats

  • A dated incident report for the water authority.
  • A FHIR R4 Bundle that conforms to the OneAquaHealth IG (hl7-eu/oah): 0 errors, 0 warnings in the HL7 validator.
  • The OneAquaHealth Citizen Science App's own submission, in the app's answer codes.

Early warning that looks forward

  • The weather either side of a visit says whether sewage came from a storm overflow or a misconnected drain.
  • The dashboard re-runs every site's latest reading with the next 48 hours' forecast: heavy rain where sewage was seen, heat on open, slow water.

Built for a riverbank

  • Works with no signal: the check is kept on the phone and sent when coverage returns.
  • Identification by shape, plain language, a printable field guide, keyboard and screen-reader support.
  • Works with no AI at all: the citizen fills the form and every reading is computed the same way.

What works, and what doesn't yet

Works, end to end

  • The guided check, the blind second opinion, the review loop and the result.
  • Both indices, fifteen One Health rules, the weather and the outlook.
  • Reports, CSV, GeoJSON, FHIR and the OneAquaHealth app export.
  • Offline recording, site trends and the dashboard. 246 automated tests, none of which need the network.

Deliberately not claimed

  • The second opinion has not been measured on real trays yet. The harness that measures it is built (EVALUATION.md); until it has run, catching mistakes is the design, not a result.
  • The sample photos' AI reading is a recording, labelled as one; any other photo is read live when a model is configured.
  • The band is a screening estimate, never a WFD classification, and every screen says so.
  • Demo sites are labelled "(demo data)". None of them is a real observation.
