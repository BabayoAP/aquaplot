IEEE OneAquaHealth Global Hackathon 2026 · Track 3, AI-Supported Assessment

You name what lives in your stream. An AI checks your answer without seeing it.

AquaPlot helps a volunteer at an urban stream work out how healthy the water is, and what that means for the people and animals nearby. You identify the animals. An AI looks at the same photo without seeing your answers and asks about anything it sees differently. The score comes from published indices and rules anyone can read.

The problem. The small animals living in a stream are the best sign of its health, but they are hard for a volunteer to name. To a beginner, a flat-headed mayfly and a stonefly look alike, and one wrong name can change a stream's reading. Track 3 says it plainly: citizen observations can be inconsistent and error-prone.

Letting an AI name the animals instead doesn't solve this. The volunteer learns nothing, nobody can tell when the AI is wrong, and a machine's guess goes into a health record.

A reading is also only useful if someone can act on it. OneAquaHealth's own Citizen Science App records the channel, banks and flow, but it doesn't ask what lives in the water.

What you'll see when you try it

  1. Two photos are already loaded.One shows a stretch of stream, the other a tray with a single insect larva (a nymph). The check is placed at OneAquaHealth research site C3 in Coimbra.
  2. You name the animal.Search "two tails" and pick a stonefly. Your answer is the one that gets scored.
  3. The AI asks you to look again.It saw the tray but not your answer. Where it sees something different, it asks a question. It can't overrule you.

"You marked some kind of stonefly. The model thinks this may be Mayfly (flat-headed). Which is it?"
How to tell: "Count the tails and look at the sides of the body. Mayfly nymphs usually have three tails and feathery or plate-like gills along the sides of the abdomen; stonefly nymphs have two tails and no gills on the abdomen."

You decide. The model never saw your answer, so when it agrees with you, that counts as a genuine second check. If you switch to its answer, AquaPlot records that you adopted it, not that you both agreed. Questions whose answer could change the stream's reading come first.

What you get at the end

A health reading, with reasons

  • A screening band on the EU Water Framework Directive scale. It comes from a published index: BMWP, or its Iberian version, IBMWP, in Portugal and Spain. The coordinates decide which one applies.
  • Findings for the stream, for people and for animals. Each one names the rule behind it, the evidence and what to do next.
  • Health alerts are held back until a person confirms the observation they rest on.

Something you can send

  • A dated incident report for the water authority.
  • A FHIR R4 Bundle, the format health systems use, that conforms to the OneAquaHealth implementation guide (hl7-eu/oah). The HL7 validator reports 0 errors and 0 warnings.
  • A submission for the OneAquaHealth Citizen Science App, in that app's own answer codes.

Warnings about what's coming

  • The weather before and after a visit helps tell whether sewage came from a storm overflow or a misconnected drain.
  • The dashboard re-checks each site's latest reading against the 48-hour forecast. It flags heavy rain where sewage was seen, and heat on open, slow-moving water.

Made for use at the stream

  • Works without signal. The check is saved on the phone and sent once you're back in coverage.
  • You identify animals by their shape, in plain language. There's a printable field guide, and the app works with a keyboard or a screen reader.
  • Works without the AI too. You fill in the form yourself, and the reading is calculated the same way.

What works and what doesn't yet

Working now

  • The guided check, the AI's blind second opinion, reviewing its questions, and the result.
  • Both indices, fifteen One Health rules, the weather and the 48-hour outlook.
  • Reports, CSV, GeoJSON, FHIR and the OneAquaHealth app export.
  • Offline recording, trends for each site, and the dashboard. There are 298 automated tests, and none of them need the network.

Not proven yet

  • Nobody has measured the second opinion on real trays yet. The setup for measuring it is built (EVALUATION.md). Until it has run, catching mistakes is what the AI is designed to do, and not yet a measured result.
  • The AI reading for the sample photos is a recording, and it's labelled as one. Any other photo is read live when a model is set up.
  • The band is a screening estimate, never an official WFD classification, and every screen says so.
  • Demo sites are marked "(demo data)". None of them is a real observation.
